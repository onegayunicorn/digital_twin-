# Quantum Avatar Simulation Lab — Design Directions

## Three candidate approaches

| Theme Name | Very Brief Intro | Probability |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Graphite Specimen Ledger | A tactile research-instrument interface with annotated materials, warm paper-like panels, and restrained cyan/amber signals. It should feel credible, curious, and calmly analytical. | 0.04 |
| Museum of Possible Physics | A bright, editorial exhibition space that treats each model as an object on display, with photographic cards and generous white space. It foregrounds scientific literacy and public learning. | 0.07 |
| Signal Garden | An organic, low-saturation system where field lines and particles gently grow through a deep earth-toned canvas. It would make the simulations feel contemplative rather than technical. | 0.03 |

## Chosen approach — Graphite Specimen Ledger

### Design Movement

**Contemporary scientific-archive editorial design** combines the materially grounded feel of an instrument bench with the hierarchy of a well-made research notebook. The interface is a *simulation record*, not a science-fiction control room.

### Core Principles

1. **Scientific boundary first.** Every speculative visual is labelled as an analogy or model before it is presented as an interaction.
2. **Material hierarchy over glow.** Graphite, mineral paper, smoked glass, and a few luminous measurement marks carry depth without the generic dark-neon aesthetic.
3. **Asymmetric reading flow.** A vertical specimen rail and wide chamber canvas create an instrument-like workspace, rather than a centred dashboard grid.
4. **Visible provenance.** Sources, model status, and simulation-only language remain close to the output rather than hidden in a footer.

### Color Philosophy

The base uses **ink-black graphite** and soft **bone paper** to distinguish the virtual chamber from the explanatory record. **Signal cyan** is reserved for correlations, status, and user focus; **amber oxide** marks analog-field traces and uncertainty. The contrast is intended to feel measured, never sensational.

### Layout Paradigm

The site is structured as a **research bench**: a narrow left index for model selection and character identity; an expansive central field canvas for the visual simulation; and a right-hand evidence ledger for telemetry, assumptions, and model results. On small screens it becomes an ordered dossier rather than a collapsed desktop dashboard.

### Signature Elements

1. **Specimen labels:** small mono labels that identify model category, scope, and confidence.
2. **Calibration ticks:** thin border ticks and scale fragments at selected edges, echoing physical lab instruments.
3. **Threaded correlations:** precise, fine cyan links between particles and avatar feature points, signifying data relationships rather than energy beams.

### Interaction Philosophy

Controls behave like a careful experiment: selecting a model updates its hypothesis, status, and visual assumptions; running a scenario starts a time-bounded, deterministic animation; reset returns the record to a named baseline. The interface never implies a connection to hardware or a real device.

### Animation

Field lines, particles, and correlation threads respond with restrained opacity and transform transitions. Scenario progression is a low-frequency sequence across five labelled stages. All motion respects `prefers-reduced-motion`; buttons acknowledge input through short scale and color transitions under 200 ms. No permanent glow pulse is used for critical content.

### Typography System

**DM Mono** supplies measurement labels, axes, and source keys. **Space Grotesk** provides the display hierarchy and interface language, with clear compact weights. Headlines are dense and left-aligned; explanatory paragraphs have a comfortable measure and a calm 1.6 line-height.

### Brand Essence

**A transparent visual laboratory for creators who want to connect avatar design with clearly bounded physics-inspired simulation.**

Personality: **precise, curious, grounded**.

### Brand Voice

Headlines sound like an experiment log rather than a promise. CTAs are direct and bounded, not promotional.

> “Trace the analogy, not the impossible claim.”

> “Run a visual model with its assumptions in view.”

### Wordmark & Logo

The wordmark uses tightly tracked Space Grotesk beside a compact **hex-cell / orbital arc / focal-dot** mark. The mark is legible as a small favicon and never depends on text at icon size.

### Signature Brand Color

**Signal Cyan — `#79D7E6`**. It appears only where the interface is communicating active correlation, selected state, or user focus.

## Style Decisions

- Major simulation visuals will read as **documented specimen apparatus**, never as portals or holographic devices. Their nearby labels, calibration cues, and bounded-model language are part of the layout rather than afterthoughts.
- **Bone paper and mineral ledger surfaces** will appear in every major region as the material of evidence and provenance, while graphite remains the material of the virtual chamber.
- The avatar is a **neutral calibrated maquette** with scan-study cues and abstract geometry, not a consumer character-creator head.
- Calibration ticks, specimen labels, and correlation threads will act as the recurring visual grammar across hero, workbench, avatar, method, and evidence sections.
